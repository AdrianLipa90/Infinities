\section{Epistemic scope and Euler/Pauli grading}
This note separates exact operator identities from physical interpretation. A bare pointed complex plane $(\C,0)$ does not uniquely determine a Hilbert space, a shift, a supercharge, or physical supersymmetry. What is exact is narrower: a specified analytic Hilbert carrier with an isometric unilateral shift and a specified $\mathbb Z_2$ grading closes to supersymmetric quantum mechanics.

Euler's sign
\[
e^{i\pi}=-1
\]
underlies the spinorial double cover. For a Pauli axis,
\[
U_j(\pi)=\exp\!\left(-\frac{i\pi}{2}\sigma_j\right)=-i\sigma_j,
\qquad U_j(\pi)^2=-I,
\qquad U_j(\pi)^4=I.
\]
Thus a projective order-two operation lifts to an order-four spinorial operation.

The companion \emph{Secret-of-a-Half} result SOH-G013 proves an exact Pauli/SU(2) lift of the projective maps
\[
R(u)=1/u,\qquad E(u)=-u,\qquad N(u)=-1/u,
\]
with representatives $i\sigma_x,i\sigma_z,i\sigma_y$ generating $Q_8$ and projecting to the Klein four group after quotienting by $\{\pm I\}$. Here we use only the grading language supplied by this lift:
\[
\Gamma=\sigma_z\otimes I,\qquad \Gamma^2=I.
\]
No statement about zeta zeros is needed for the constructions below.
