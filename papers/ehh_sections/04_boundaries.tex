\[
\boxed{

\[

These are separate constructions sharing Pauli/spinorial language. No additional identification is required for either theorem to stand.

\section{Falsification boundaries}

The following statements are \emph{not} consequences of the present mathematics.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{F\arabic*.}]
\item A bare pointed complex plane does not determine a unique Fredholm operator or supercharge.
\item Tetrahedral geometry alone does not derive fermionic CAR modes.
\item The existence of the mathematical supersymmetry algebras does not establish that a corresponding supersymmetry is realized by Nature.
\item The two supersymmetry bricks need not arise from a single physical model.
\end{enumerate}

The useful content is therefore structural and testable: each brick comes with explicit hypotheses, exact algebra, a topological or group-theoretic invariant, and a clear boundary at which the theorem stops.

\section{Conclusion}

The strongest compact identity of the operator sector is
\[
\boxed{
\Delta_W(T_f)
=
\indop(T_f)
=
-\operatorname{wind}(f).
}
\]
It immediately contains the Hilbert Hotel,
\[
\Delta_W(S^k)=-k,
\]
and for a finite Blaschke product,
\[
\boxed{
\#Z_{\mathbb D}(B)
=
-\Delta_W(T_B).
}
\]
Hence Euler phase winding, Fredholm defect, Hilbert-Hotel deficiency, analytic zero multiplicity, and protected supersymmetric zero-mode imbalance are different realizations of one integer within the stated Hardy/Toeplitz model.

Independently, the tetrahedral sector is no longer an arbitrary four-ray choice: it is the minimal qubit SIC, exactly the projective Pauli orbit, with $Q_8$ as its spinorial core and $2T$ as its full orientation-preserving binary symmetry. Once the CAR sector is supplied, the resulting $\mathcal N=1$ supertranslation frame is equivariant under that symmetry.

The programme is therefore best developed as modular supersymmetry: prove each component at full strength, then compose only when an additional bridge is itself proved.
